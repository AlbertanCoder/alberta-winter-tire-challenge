# How the physics works (for engineers)

The v2 model lives in [`js/physics.js`](js/physics.js): pure JavaScript, no DOM, loads in the browser and in Node.
Calibration is in [`docs/CALIBRATION.md`](docs/CALIBRATION.md) (generated by `npm run calibrate`); tests are in
[`test/physics.test.js`](test/physics.test.js) (`npm test`). **Status: Phase 1 work in progress; see PROGRESS.md.**
Every number below has a source or is labelled *estimate*.

## Frames
- World: x, z on the ground, y up. Heading ψ; forward = (−sin ψ, −cos ψ); left = (−cos ψ, sin ψ). ψ grows turning left.
- Body: X forward, Y left; yaw rate r = dψ/dt. Wheels 0 FL, 1 FR, 2 RL, 3 RR at (a, ±t_f/2) and (−b, ±t_r/2).
- Road: s along the road, off positive to the right of the centre line.

## Rigid body (road plane)
m(u̇ − v r) = ΣF_X,  m(v̇ + u r) = ΣF_Y,  I_z ṙ = ΣM_z. Fixed step Δt = 1 ms, semi-implicit Euler, deterministic.

Forces: the four tire forces (rotated through each front wheel's steer angle δ), gravity along the road surface,
drag, crosswind, ruts and ploughing.

## Normal loads
Total normal load N = m g / √(1 + |∇h|²), where h(x, z) is the ground height under the car (grade, crown, banking).
Static split front/rear by b/L and a/L, then:
- longitudinal transfer ΔF = m a_x h / L,
- lateral transfer front ΔF_f = k_f m a_y h / t_f, rear ΔF_r = (1 − k_f) m a_y h / t_r,

with a_x, a_y the *specific force* (what an accelerometer reads: everything but gravity), low-pass filtered with
τ = 0.05 s (*estimate*, stands in for suspension lag; it also avoids an algebraic loop). Negative loads are clipped to
zero and the four are rescaled so they always sum to N (test 10).

## Tires
**Slip with relaxation length** (low-speed treatment after Bernard & Clover, SAE 950311). Each wheel carries
deflection states u_x, u_y:
 u̇_x = (Rω − v_x) − (|v_x|/L_x) u_x,  u̇_y = −v_y − (|v_x|/L_y) u_y,
so κ = u_x/L_x and α = u_y/L_y settle to the slip ratio and tan(slip angle) at speed, and act like a stiff spring at
standstill (no creep, no jitter). Both are integrated implicitly (stable at any speed). L_x = 0.15 m, L_y = 0.40 m
(*estimates*). Below 1.5 m/s a damping term blends in (damping ratio 0.7, *estimate*).

**Wheel spin**: I_w ω̇ = T_drive − T_brake − R F_x, solved implicitly together with u_x using the tire's secant
stiffness (wheel spin is the stiff part of the system). Brake torque acts as Coulomb friction, so a braked wheel at rest
stays at rest until the torque needed to hold it exceeds the brake's.

**Combined slip, one grip budget** (Magic Formula style, after Pacejka, *Tire and Vehicle Dynamics*):
 s = √(κ² + α²)/s_pk,  F = μ F_z · sin(C·atan(B s)),  B = tan(π/(2C)),  (F_x, F_y) = F · (κ, α)/√(κ² + α²).
The curve peaks at s = 1 and falls toward sin(Cπ/2) as the tire slides. The force always points against the slip, so
a locked or sliding tire pushes straight back along its sliding direction and **locked front wheels cannot steer**
(test 4). Gravel uses C = 1 with a fitted B: no peak, because a locked tire ploughs a wedge of gravel and grips more
(NHTSA, Forkenbrock et al., SAE 1999-01-1287).
- **Small-slip stiffness is measured, not scaled with grip**: stiffness ÷ load from Svendenius (2007), *Validation of
  the Brush Model towards VTI-measurement data recorded in Arjeplog 2006*, Lund University TFRT-7617, Table 8
  (winter tire, cornering, "Norm" column) at 2 / 4 / 6 kN: snow 11.9 / 13.4 / 13.7, ice 8.9 / 7.6 / 5.5, wet asphalt
  29.6 / 28.6 / 22.38 (interpolated by each wheel's load; end values held outside 2–6 kN). Its longitudinal values
  (Table 5) are close, so one value serves both directions. The unit is per unit slip (stiffness normalised by load,
  as in the report's brush model; inferred, the report doesn't spell it out). Mapping (*estimates*): dry uses the
  wet-asphalt row (no dry row in the source); fresh snow and slush use the snow row.
- Peak slip then follows: s_pk = μ · slope0 / stiffness, with slope0 = C·B the curve's initial slope. Ice is about
  5× less grippy than snow but only about 1.8× less stiff, so it peaks at a much smaller slip: it feels normal until
  it lets go. Gravel keeps a fixed peak slip of 0.25 (*estimate*) with its fitted no-peak curve.
- Sideways peak μ = braking peak μ (no opened source supports a difference).
- Load sensitivity: μ × (1 − 0.1 (F_z/F_z0 − 1)), F_z0 = the vehicle's average wheel load (*estimate*).
- μ and C per tire × surface come from calibration (below). Temperature, tread and vehicle factors scale μ exactly as
  the v1 tables do (`COND`, `TMUL`, `HARD`, `TREAD`, `VEH.f` in [`js/grip.js`](js/grip.js)).

## Calibration (see docs/CALIBRATION.md)
1. Table values that don't reproduce their own measured source within 2% are fixed: all-season on packed snow
   0.25 → 0.2362 (Tire Rack 2026: 84.5 ft from 40 km/h; v1 gave 80.0 ft).
2. For each tire × surface on the reference small car at −10 °C: μ_pk and C are fitted so the full simulation's
   straight-line stops from 19, 40, 60, 80, 100 km/h match the v1 `stopRun()` (2% with ABS, 3% locked).
3. Calibration stops run in D, so idle creep acts below 1.7 m/s, as in a real automatic (the v1 model had none;
   the fit absorbs it).
4. Checked against the measured anchors: Tire Rack packed snow from 40 km/h (56.1 / 58.6 / 84.5 ft) and the
   ice-rink ratio (50.6 ÷ 38.7 ft).

## Drivetrain
- Engine (shift points 2,200-5,200 rpm up, 1,300 rpm down, driveline efficiency 0.9: estimates): torque = throttle × min(T_max, P_max/ω_e), ω_e = max(idle, wheel speed × gear × final drive); fuel cut at
  redline. Automatic gearbox with speed-based shifts. Idle creep in D: up to 0.03 m g toward 1.7 m/s (*estimates*).
- Open differential on each driven axle (equal torque left and right), so one wheel on ice limits drive (test 7).
- AWD (simplification): an active coupling shifts torque toward the axle slipping less.
- 4×4: locked centre (torque split so both axles accelerate alike, plus a correction on their speed difference).
- No torque converter multiplication and no engine braking (simplifications).

## Brakes and driver aids (generic logic, not any maker's)
- Pedal → torque; full pedal = 1.5 m g R total (*estimate*: enough to lock all four on dry).
- ABS off: fixed 70/30 front split (*estimate*), so the fronts lock first; locked rears make the car spin (test 5).
- ABS on: electronic brake-force distribution (ideal split for the measured deceleration) plus per-wheel slip-band
  control: release above 10.5% slip or when the filtered wheel deceleration passes 15 m/s² (lock-up coming),
  reapply below 9.5%, hold if the slip is already moving the right way fast; off below 1 m/s (*estimates*).
- ESC: target yaw rate from the steering angle (bicycle model with a stored understeer gradient), limited by the
  friction the lateral accelerometer shows. Too much yaw → brake the outside front; too little → brake the inside
  rear; engine torque cut. It uses only steering angle, yaw rate, lateral acceleration and speed; never the surface μ.
  ESC and TCS need the ABS hardware, so ABS off turns them off too. Checked with the FMVSS/CMVSS 126 sine-with-dwell
  procedure (49 CFR 571.126) on dry pavement (test 3; `scripts/fmvss126.js`).
- TCS: brakes a driven wheel that spins faster than the car and cuts engine torque.
- Simplification: ABS, ESC and TCS use the car's true forward speed as their reference; real units estimate it
  (this is optimistic on ice with all four wheels near lock-up).
- ESC's reference model uses each vehicle's own understeer gradient, measured by `npm run calibrate`
  (CALIBRATION.md section 5), as a real ESC is tuned to its car.

## Road and air
- Gravity along the surface from the height gradient ∇h at the CG; per-wheel gradient differences (ruts, ridges)
  add a sideways share of that wheel's load. Crown and banking come through the same gradient.
- Drag: 0.05 + 0.0004 v² m/s² (the v1 coast-down formula, kept so calibration stays comparable; a simplification).
  Applied to the effective mass m + 4 I_w/R² because a coast-down deceleration includes the spinning wheels.
- Crosswind: F = ½ ρ C_y A |V_rel| w_side with C_y = 0.7, A = 2.2 L m², centre of pressure 0.25 L ahead of the CG
  (*estimates*); ρ = 1.34 kg/m³ (ideal gas at −10 °C, sea-level pressure).
- Ploughing drag in slush or deep snow: a share of the wheel's load against its motion (*estimate*, per scenario).

## What the model can't do
- No suspension, roll or pitch dynamics (load transfer uses filtered accelerations), and no tire heating or wear.
- Flat-plane model: the road's slope enters only through gravity and load, not through 3-D body motion.
- Generic ABS, ESC, TCS and AWD logic; real systems differ by maker and are tuned far more finely.
- No Ackermann steering geometry, no camber or aligning torque, no tire relaxation differences by surface.
- Peak-slip values, relaxation lengths, load sensitivity, ruts, ridges and gusts are estimates.
- Deterministic per JavaScript engine: Math.sin/atan may differ in the last bits between browsers, so identical
  inputs give identical runs in the same browser, not necessarily bit-identical across browsers.
- The cars come out only mildly understeering (0.02–0.33 deg/g, CALIBRATION.md section 5): the model has no
  suspension compliance or aligning torque, which add understeer on real cars.
- A physics model calibrated to published tests, not a guarantee of how any tire, car or road will behave.
